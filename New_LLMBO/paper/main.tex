\begin{abstract}
Fast-charging protocol design requires balancing charging time, thermal stress, and degradation when each candidate protocol is expensive to evaluate. Bayesian optimization (BO) is suited to this setting, but its early surrogate is built from few observations and cannot directly use qualitative battery knowledge. We introduce LLMBO-MO, a multi-objective BO framework that uses a large language model (LLM) as a bounded search adviser rather than as an evaluator. LLM proposals are repaired and screened before initialization; during optimization, accepted point or region preferences modify only the acquisition-time Gaussian-process mean, with deterministic fallback to expected improvement. Every selected protocol is evaluated by the electrochemical--thermal--aging simulator before entering the BO data set. We evaluate five paired seeds with 56 simulator calls per run on the Chen2020 and Ecker2015 parameterizations. On Chen2020, LLMBO-MO obtains a benchmark-scaled hypervolume of $0.3835\pm0.0079$, compared with $0.3853\pm0.0094$ for ParEGO, giving similar mean quality with lower variation. On Ecker2015, it reaches $1.8684\pm0.0027$ versus $1.5866\pm0.0130$ and improves all five paired runs. A matched four-way ablation attributes the largest mean gain to LLM warm starting; the full configuration has a comparable mean and the lowest seed-to-seed deviation. The method therefore provides controlled use of language-based priors, with cell-dependent gains and additional wall-clock cost rather than uniform dominance over numerical BO.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Fast charging is central to the practical adoption of electric vehicles, but it cannot be pursued independently of safety and lifetime. Aggressive currents shorten charging time while increasing heat generation and accelerating degradation mechanisms, so a useful protocol must negotiate competing objectives rather than optimize speed alone~\cite{ref1,ref2}. In this work, a protocol is defined by the currents and state-of-charge allocations of a multistage charge, and its quality is assessed by charging time, peak temperature rise, and capacity fade under operational constraints.

Searching this protocol space is difficult because the objective map is nonlinear, constrained, and expensive to evaluate. Electrochemical--thermal--aging models provide a safer and less costly test bed than repeated cell experiments, but they still limit the number of protocols that can be explored~\cite{ref3}. Bayesian optimization (BO) addresses this regime by using a probabilistic surrogate to select informative evaluations~\cite{ref4}. Its value for battery charging has also been demonstrated in closed-loop experiments, where combining early prediction with BO sharply reduced the time needed to identify long-life fast-charging protocols~\cite{ref15}.

Multi-objective protocol design adds a further difficulty: improving one metric can move the solution away from another. Evolutionary algorithms such as NSGA-II approximate a Pareto front through population-based search~\cite{ref6}, whereas ParEGO converts different trade-off directions into a sequence of scalar BO problems~\cite{ref5}. Data-driven and physics-informed battery optimization methods provide additional ways to exploit measured or modeled structure~\cite{ref7,ref8}. Nevertheless, when only a few evaluations are available, the surrogate is determined mainly by sparse observations from the current run; useful qualitative knowledge about plausible charging profiles is not naturally expressed in those observations.

Recent work has therefore explored LLMs as knowledge-bearing components in BO. LLAMBO uses an LLM for warm starting, surrogate-related reasoning, and candidate generation, with its largest benefits reported early in the search~\cite{ref9}. BORA instead asks an LLM to identify promising regions while retaining a stochastic optimizer as the numerical backbone~\cite{ref10}. These results show the potential of language-based priors, but they also expose an important design risk: an LLM recommendation is neither a physical measurement nor a calibrated posterior observation. Independent evaluations further show that directly prompted LLM agents may fail to use experimental feedback reliably, motivating hybrid designs that separate prior knowledge from data-driven acquisition~\cite{ref16}. Preference-guided BO has recently taken this direction by translating LLM region preferences into controlled surrogate-mean adjustments~\cite{ref17}.

Applying this idea to battery fast charging is not a direct substitution. Guidance must change with the current multi-objective trade-off and the evolving Pareto set; proposed protocols must satisfy stage and state-of-charge constraints; and an invalid recommendation must not override numerical uncertainty or interrupt the optimization loop. These requirements lead to the central principle of this work: the LLM should provide bounded and rejectable search preferences, while numerical acquisition, constraint handling, and simulator evaluation retain final control.

Based on this principle, we propose LLMBO-MO, a two-touchpoint framework for controlled knowledge augmentation of decomposition-based multi-objective BO. Before BO begins, the LLM proposes domain-informed protocols that are repaired, screened, and selected as a feasible and diverse initial portfolio. During optimization, it receives the current trade-off direction and optimization history and returns a point or region preference. Following the region-lifted preference idea~\cite{ref17}, the preference modifies only the mean used to rank acquisition candidates; its influence is bounded and decays as numerical evidence accumulates. Failed parsing, low confidence, or constraint violations trigger deterministic fallbacks, and every selected protocol is evaluated by the battery simulator before it enters the BO database.

Our simulator study uses five paired seeds and 56 evaluations per run on two cell parameterizations. On Chen2020, LLMBO-MO and ParEGO reach similar mean scaled hypervolume (0.3835 and 0.3853, respectively), with lower variation for LLMBO-MO. On Ecker2015, LLMBO-MO improves the five-seed mean from 1.5866 to 1.8684 and wins every paired seed. A matched four-way ablation identifies warm starting as the largest average contributor, while the full configuration has the lowest seed-to-seed deviation. LLM calls increase wall-clock time, so the study concerns solution quality under a simulator budget rather than computational speed.

The main contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A battery-specific, two-touchpoint LLM--MOBO framework that uses language-based knowledge at initialization and during search without replacing the numerical optimizer.
\item A constraint-aware warm-start portfolio that combines LLM proposals with repair, feasibility screening, diversity selection, and deterministic fallback.
\item A multi-objective adaptation of region-lifted guidance that conditions preferences on the current trade-off and optimization history, limits their acquisition-time influence, and prevents unreliable guidance from directly controlling acquisition.
\item A five-seed fixed-budget study on Chen2020 and Ecker2015, including benchmark-scaled HV trajectories, Pareto-set and charging-profile inspection, and a matched four-way ablation of warm starting and iteration-level guidance.
\end{enumerate}

Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes the paper.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Charging Protocol Design as CMOP}

The charging protocol design problem determines the optimal parameters of a multi-stage constant-current (MCC) charging protocol through optimization. It is formulated as a CMOP:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\btheta} \; f(\btheta) = \{t_c(\btheta),\; \Delta T_p(\btheta),\; Q_s(\btheta)\}
\label{eq:cmop}
\end{equation}
subject to:
\begin{align}
\Delta s_1 + \Delta s_2 &\leq 0.70 \label{eq:dsoc_constr}\\
V_{\text{peak}}(\btheta) &\leq V_{\max} \label{eq:v_constr}\\
T_{\text{peak}}(\btheta) &\leq T_{\max} \label{eq:t_constr}
\end{align}
where $\btheta$ denotes the decision vector, $t_c$ is the total charging time (seconds), $\Delta T_p$ is the peak temperature rise above ambient (Kelvin), and $Q_s$ represents the capacity fade (\%). The constraints enforce that the cumulative SOC intervals leave room for the final stage~\eqref{eq:dsoc_constr}, the terminal voltage stays within bounds~\eqref{eq:v_constr}, and the cell temperature remains below the safety limit~\eqref{eq:t_constr}.

\subsection{Decision Variables and Constraints}

A 3-stage constant-current (3-CC) protocol is adopted. The decision vector comprises five variables:
\begin{equation}
\btheta = [I_1,\; I_2,\; I_3,\; \Delta s_1,\; \Delta s_2]^\top
\label{eq:theta}
\end{equation}
where $I_1, I_2, I_3$ denote the charging currents for stages 1, 2, and 3, respectively, and $\Delta s_1, \Delta s_2$ denote the state-of-charge (SOC) interval widths for stages 1 and 2. The bounds and physical interpretation are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:variables}.

\begin{table}[!t]
\caption{Decision Variables, Bounds, and Physical Meaning}
\label{tab:variables}
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Variable & Symbol & Bounds & Unit \\
\midrule
Stage-1 current & $I_1$ & $[2.0,\; 6.0]$ & A \\
Stage-2 current & $I_2$ & $[2.0,\; 5.0]$ & A \\
Stage-3 current & $I_3$ & $[2.0,\; 3.0]$ & A \\
Stage-1 SOC width & $\Delta s_1$ & $[0.10,\; 0.40]$ & -- \\
Stage-2 SOC width & $\Delta s_2$ & $[0.10,\; 0.30]$ & -- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The constraint $\Delta s_1 + \Delta s_2 \leq 0.70$ ensures $\Delta s_3 = 0.80 - \Delta s_1 - \Delta s_2 > 0.10$ for the final charging stage. A practical soft constraint $\Delta s_1 + \Delta s_2 \leq 0.65$ is enforced during LLM candidate generation to maintain a safety margin.

A monotone current profile $I_1 \geq I_2 \geq I_3$ is recommended on electrochemical grounds---higher currents during early SOC stages (low lithium concentration in the negative electrode) are less likely to induce lithium plating than the same currents at higher SOC~\cite{ref2}.

\section{Electrochemical-Thermal-Aging Model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Electrochemical Model}

The Single Particle Model with electrolyte (SPMe)~\cite{ref11} is adopted as a computationally efficient reduced-order variant of the Doyle--Fuller--Newman (P2D) model. The SPMe approximates each electrode as a single spherical particle while retaining an electrolyte concentration equation, yielding a favorable trade-off between physical fidelity and computational cost. The model is implemented using the PyBaMM open-source platform~\cite{ref12} with the Chen2020~\cite{ref13} parameter set for the LG INR21700-M50 cell (5~Ah, NMC811/graphite).

For cross-validation, the Ecker2015~\cite{ref14} parameter set for the Kokam SLPB~75106100 cell is also supported.

\subsection{Thermal Model}

A lumped-parameter thermal model is coupled with the electrochemical model. The governing equation for the cell temperature $T$ is:
\begin{equation}
\rho C_p \frac{\partial T}{\partial t} = q_{\text{gen}} - h_c (T - T_{\text{air}})
\label{eq:thermal}
\end{equation}
where $q_{\text{gen}}$ is the volumetric heat generation rate computed from the electrochemical model (ohmic + reaction heat), $\rho$ is the cell density, $C_p$ is the specific heat capacity, and $h_c$ is the convective heat transfer coefficient. The temperature alignment factor $\kappa_T = 1.10$ is applied to the peak temperature rise objective to account for model--experiment discrepancy.

\subsection{Aging Model}

The capacity fade percentage is computed as a function of the mean SOC, mean temperature, and mean current during the charging process:
\begin{equation}
Q_s = \frac{Q_{\text{eff}}}{\text{Cap}(\bar{s},\; \bar{T},\; \bar{I})} \times 100\%
\label{eq:aging}
\end{equation}
where $\text{Cap}(\cdot)$ is an Arrhenius-type function capturing the dependence of cycle life on operating conditions, and $Q_{\text{eff}}$ is the effective charge throughput. The temperature input to the aging model uses a separate alignment factor $\kappa_a = 0.61$ to match the thermal exposure relevant to SEI growth kinetics.

\subsection{Simulation Pipeline}

Given a protocol $\btheta$, the simulator executes three sequential stages:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Stage decomposition:} Compute stage boundaries as $\text{SOC}_k = \text{SOC}_{k-1} + \Delta s_k$ for $k=1,2,3$, with $\Delta s_3 = 0.80 - \Delta s_1 - \Delta s_2$.
\item \textbf{Stage-wise solving:} For each stage $k$, apply current $I_k$ over the SOC interval $[\text{SOC}_{k-1},\, \text{SOC}_k]$ using the SPMe model with thermal coupling.
\item \textbf{Objective extraction:} Compute $t_c = \sum_k t_k$, $\Delta T_p = \max(T) - T_{\text{init}}$, and $Q_s$ from the aging model.
\end{enumerate}
Infeasible protocols (e.g., voltage exceeding $V_{\max} = 4.4$~V or temperature exceeding $T_{\max} = 328.15$~K) are assigned a penalty objective vector, ensuring the optimizer avoids these regions.

\section{Proposed LLMBO-MO Framework}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Motivation and Framework Overview}

The key insight of LLMBO-MO is that LLM-generated domain knowledge should not replace the surrogate model, but rather complement it at strategic points where it can have the greatest impact. This leads to a two-touchpoint architecture (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}):

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Touchpoint 1 (Initialization):} The LLM generates domain-informed warm-start candidates. A diversity-aware portfolio selector~(Section~\ref{sec:warmstart}) constructs the initial design of experiments from the LLM pool.
\item \textbf{Touchpoint 2 (Iteration-Level Guidance):} The LLM provides structured preferences (point or region mode). These are translated into a bounded, decay-annealed acquisition-time mean shift through the region-lifted GP mechanism~(Section~\ref{sec:coupling}).
\end{itemize}

\input{fig_llambo_mo_framework}

At both touchpoints, invalid LLM outputs are handled by deterministic fallbacks, ensuring that the optimization loop remains a standard BO loop even when LLM guidance is unavailable.

\subsection{Riesz $s$-Energy Weight Generation and Tchebycheff Scalarization}
\label{sec:scalarization}

\subsubsection{Objective Transformation}

The objectives are transformed to improve numerical conditioning:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{f}_1 = \log_{10}(t_c), \quad \tilde{f}_2 = \Delta T_p, \quad \tilde{f}_3 = \log_{10}(Q_s)
\label{eq:log_transform}
\end{equation}
Log-transformation is applied to $t_c$ and $Q_s$ to compress the dynamic range, while $\Delta T_p$ is retained in raw Kelvin-rise units.

\subsubsection{Ideal-Point Gap Normalization}

Let $\mathbf{z}^* = (\tilde{f}_1^*, \tilde{f}_2^*, \tilde{f}_3^*)$ denote the ideal point in the log-transformed space, computed from a pre-defined reference. For each observation $\btheta_j$, the normalized gap to the ideal point is:
\begin{equation}
\bar{f}_i(\btheta_j) = \frac{|\tilde{f}_i(\btheta_j) - z_i^*|}{y_{\max,i} - y_{\min,i}}, \quad i = 1,2,3
\label{eq:normalize}
\end{equation}
where $y_{\min,i}, y_{\max,i}$ are the dynamic bounds of the log-transformed objectives, with a 5\% minimum-range floor enforced using global reference bounds to avoid near-zero denominators.

\subsubsection{Augmented Tchebycheff Scalarization}

Given a weight vector $\bw = (w_1, w_2, w_3)$ on the 2-simplex, the scalarized objective is:
\begin{equation}
g^{\text{tch}}(\btheta \mid \bw) = \max_{i} \{w_i \cdot \bar{f}_i(\btheta)\} + \eta \sum_{i=1}^{3} w_i \cdot \bar{f}_i(\btheta)
\label{eq:tcheby}
\end{equation}
where $\eta = 0.05$ is the augmentation coefficient that ensures the scalarized function is strictly convex in the weight space.

\subsubsection{Riesz $s$-Energy Weight Set}

To ensure uniform coverage of the Pareto front, the weight set $\bW = \{\bw^{(1)}, \ldots, \bw^{(N_w)}\}$ is generated by minimizing the Riesz $s$-energy on the 2-simplex:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\bw^{(1)}, \ldots, \bw^{(N_w)}} \sum_{i < j} \|\bw^{(i)} - \bw^{(j)}\|^{-s}, \quad s = 2.0
\label{eq:riesz}
\end{equation}
solved via gradient descent with 300 iterations and learning rate $5 \times 10^{-3}$. The resulting weight set is cached and reused across runs. At each iteration, a weight is sampled from $\bW$ without replacement (cycling mode).

\subsection{Gaussian Process Surrogate Model}

LLMBO-MO employs a Gaussian process (GP) as the surrogate model on the scalarized objective $g^{\text{tch}}$. The kernel function is the Mat\'{e}rn $5/2$ kernel with automatic relevance determination (ARD):
\begin{equation}
k(\btheta, \btheta') = \sigma_f^2 \left(1 + \frac{\sqrt{5}r}{\ell} + \frac{5r^2}{3\ell^2}\right) \exp\left(-\frac{\sqrt{5}r}{\ell}\right)
\label{eq:matern}
\end{equation}
where $r = \|\btheta - \btheta'\|_\Lambda$ is the Mahalanobis distance with ARD length scales $\ell = (\ell_1, \ldots, \ell_5)$, and $\sigma_f^2$ is the signal variance. A white noise kernel $\sigma_n^2 \delta(\btheta, \btheta')$ is added for numerical stability. The GP is fitted on input-normalized 5D variables (each dimension scaled to $[0,1]$).

Hyperparameters are optimized by maximizing the log marginal likelihood with 5 random restarts. The GP posterior at a test point $\btheta$ is:
\begin{align}
\mu(\btheta) &= \mathbf{k}_*^\top (K + \sigma_n^2 I)^{-1} \mathbf{y} \label{eq:gp_mean}\\
\sigma^2(\btheta) &= k(\btheta, \btheta) - \mathbf{k}_*^\top (K + \sigma_n^2 I)^{-1} \mathbf{k}_* \label{eq:gp_var}
\end{align}

\subsection{LLM Integration: Two-Touchpoint Architecture}

\subsubsection{Touchpoint 1: Warmstart Candidate Generation with Portfolio Selection}
\label{sec:warmstart}

Instead of relying on random sampling for initialization, LLMBO-MO queries the LLM to generate an initial pool of $n_{\text{pool}}$ candidate charging protocols. The LLM is prompted with:
\begin{itemize}
\item Battery specifications (cell type, capacity, electrode chemistry)
\item Decision variable bounds and the dSOC constraint
\item Electrochemical design principles (monotone current profile, safety margins)
\item Request for $n_{\text{pool}}$ diverse protocol recommendations
\end{itemize}

From the LLM-generated pool $\mathcal{C}_{\text{LLM}}$, a \textbf{diversity-aware portfolio selector} chooses $n_{\text{ws}}$ candidates by minimizing the composite loss
\begin{align}
J(\btheta) &=
-\omega_d d_{\min}(\btheta,\mathcal{C}_{\text{sel}})
+\omega_p p_{\text{soft}}(\btheta)
+\omega_m p_{\text{mono}}(\btheta),
\label{eq:portfolio}\\
p_{\text{soft}}(\btheta) &= \max(0,\Delta s_1+\Delta s_2-0.65), \nonumber\\
p_{\text{mono}}(\btheta) &= \max(I_2-I_1,0)+\max(I_3-I_2,0). \nonumber
\end{align}
Here $d_{\min}(\cdot)$ is the minimum Euclidean distance to already-selected candidates. The first term encourages coverage, while the latter two terms penalize soft dSOC violations and non-monotone current profiles. The greedy selection proceeds iteratively, ensuring that the initial design spans the feasible region rather than clustering around a single mode.

If the LLM fails to return sufficient valid candidates, a physics-informed fallback generates 15 hand-crafted prior points covering the Pareto-extreme directions (fast-charging, temperature-safe, aging-minimized) plus Latin Hypercube Sampling for remaining slots.

\subsubsection{Touchpoint 2: Iteration-Level Guidance}
\label{sec:guidance}

At each BO iteration, after the GP is fitted, the LLM is queried for guidance on where to search next. The prompt includes:
\begin{itemize}
\item Current optimization state: iteration $t/T$, weight vector $\bw^{(t)}$, best protocol, stagnation count
\item Pareto front context: top-$k$ and recent observations from the database
\item Few-shot examples: best-3 and worst-2 scalarized protocols from history
\end{itemize}

The LLM responds with a structured preference in one of two modes:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Point mode:} A single 5D protocol $\btheta_{\text{pref}}$ with confidence $c \in [0,1]$.
\item \textbf{Region mode:} A preferred sub-region defined by lower and upper bounds $(\mathbf{lb}, \mathbf{ub})$ with confidence $c$, optionally accompanied by mechanistic reasoning and risk flags.
\end{itemize}

A confidence-weighted, mode-specific fallback generates heuristic guidance when the LLM response is invalid. The fallback shifts the search center based on the dominant weight direction and expands the region width with stagnation.

\subsection{GP-LLM Coupling via Region-Lifted Mean Shift}
\label{sec:coupling}

Following preference-guided BO~\cite{ref17}, the core mechanism for integrating LLM iteration guidance with the GP surrogate is a \textbf{region-lifted GP} coupling adapted to the present multi-objective battery setting. Given a valid LLM preference, LLMBO-MO constructs an acquisition-time mean shift that changes candidate ranking without refitting the GP or changing its posterior variance. If parsing, confidence, feasibility, or guard checks fail, the optimizer uses the unshifted GP/EI acquisition.

\subsubsection{Grid Construction and Weighting}

For a point preference at $\btheta_{\text{pref}}$, a local grid $\bG = \{\btheta_g^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^{n_g}$ is constructed around $\btheta_{\text{pref}}$ with scale $\sigma_{\text{local}}$, and weights $\mathbf{v} \in \R^{n_g}$ are assigned via an EI-softmax distribution over the grid:
\begin{equation}
v_j = \frac{\exp(\alpha_{\text{EI}}(\btheta_g^{(j)}) / \tau_{\text{anchor}})}{\sum_{k=1}^{n_g} \exp(\alpha_{\text{EI}}(\btheta_g^{(k)}) / \tau_{\text{anchor}})}
\label{eq:anchor_weights}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_{\text{anchor}} = 0.35$ is the anchor temperature.

For a region preference $(\mathbf{lb}, \mathbf{ub})$, the grid points are uniformly sampled within the region with uniform weights.

\subsubsection{Coupling Strength $\lambda$}

The coupling strength is computed as:
\begin{equation}
\lambda = \text{clip}\!\left(\frac{c \cdot \kappa_c}{\sqrt{\mathbf{v}^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\bG\bG} \mathbf{v}}} \cdot \rho^{t},\; \lambda_{\min},\; \lambda_{\max}\right)
\label{eq:lambda}
\end{equation}
where $c$ is the LLM confidence, $\kappa_c$ is an optional confidence scaling factor, $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\bG\bG}$ is the GP covariance matrix over the grid, $\rho \in [0,1)$ is the decay rate, and $t$ is the iteration index. The decay $\rho^t$ ensures that LLM influence diminishes as the GP accumulates sufficient data, preventing late-stage bias.

\subsubsection{Acquisition-Time Mean Shift}

For any candidate point $\btheta$, the coupled posterior mean is:
\begin{align}
\mu_{\text{c}}(\btheta) &=
\mu(\btheta)-\lambda\,g_{\text{gate}}\,
m_{\text{local}}(\btheta)\,q(\btheta)\,\hat{\sigma}_y,
\label{eq:coupled_mean}\\
q(\btheta) &= [\mathbf{k}_{\btheta,\bG}\mathbf{v}]_z . \nonumber
\end{align}
where:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathbf{k}_{\btheta,\bG}$ is the kernel (prior or posterior) covariance vector between $\btheta$ and the grid $\bG$, computed in the GP-standardized target space.
\item $g_{\text{gate}} \in [0,1]$ is a multi-factor gating signal that combines alignment, history, hypervolume improvement, and stage scores.
\item $m_{\text{local}}(\btheta)$ is a spatial mask that smoothly decays outside the preferred region (for region mode) or away from the preferred point (for point mode):
\end{itemize}
\begin{align}
m_{\text{local}}(\btheta) &=
\begin{cases}
m_{\text{pt}}(\btheta), & \text{point mode},\\
m_{\text{reg}}(\btheta), & \text{region mode},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:local_mask}\\
m_{\text{pt}}(\btheta) &=
\exp\!\left[-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{5}
\frac{(\theta_i-\theta_{\text{pref},i})^2}{\sigma_{\text{local},i}^2}\right],
\nonumber\\
m_{\text{reg}}(\btheta) &=
\begin{cases}
1, & \btheta \in [\mathbf{lb}, \mathbf{ub}],\\
\exp\!\left[-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{5}\frac{\delta_i^2}{w_i^2}\right],
& \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\nonumber
\end{align}
where $\delta_i = \max(\mathbf{lb}_i - \theta_i,\; 0) + \max(\theta_i - \mathbf{ub}_i,\; 0)$ and $w_i = (\mathbf{ub}_i - \mathbf{lb}_i)/2$. The term $\hat{\sigma}_y$ is the GP target standard deviation, converting the shift from standardized to raw target space.

The posterior variance $\sigma^2(\btheta)$ is unchanged; the coupling modifies only the acquisition-time mean, preserving the GP uncertainty estimate used by EI.

\subsubsection{Prior vs.\ Posterior Covariance Source}

The shift source can be configured to use either the \emph{prior kernel} (which reflects the GP's length-scale structure without conditioning on data) or the \emph{posterior covariance} (which is data-informed). The prior-kernel source is more stable in early iterations when data are sparse, while the posterior source provides sharper, data-informed shifts in later iterations.

\subsection{Risk-Aware Acquisition Function}
\label{sec:acquisition}

\subsubsection{Expected Improvement}

LLMBO-MO uses the Expected Improvement (EI) acquisition function. Given the acquisition-time mean $\mu_{\text{c}}$, the EI at a candidate point $\btheta$ is:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{\text{EI}}(\btheta) = (f_{\min} - \mu_{\text{c}}(\btheta))\, \Phi(z) + \sigma(\btheta)\, \phi(z)
\label{eq:ei}
\end{equation}
where $z = (f_{\min} - \mu_{\text{c}}(\btheta)) / \sigma(\btheta)$, and $\Phi, \phi$ are the standard normal CDF and PDF.

\subsubsection{Acquisition Prior: Guidance Bonus and Risk Penalty}

The final acquisition score combines normalized EI with an acquisition prior:
\begin{equation}
\alpha(\btheta) = \hat{\alpha}_{\text{EI}}(\btheta) + \beta_{\text{guid}}(\btheta) - \beta_{\text{risk}}(\btheta)
\label{eq:acq_prior}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\alpha}_{\text{EI}} = \text{z-score}(\log(1 + \alpha_{\text{EI}}))$ is the normalized log-EI, and:

\textbf{Guidance bonus} $\beta_{\text{guid}}$: For region-mode guidance, a Gaussian bonus centered on the preferred region; for point-mode, a Gaussian centered on the preferred point with scale $\sigma_{\text{guid}}$:
\begin{equation}
\beta_{\text{guid}}(\btheta) = \alpha_{\text{guid}} \cdot \exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{5} \frac{(\theta_i - c_i)^2}{\sigma_i^2}\right)
\label{eq:guid_bonus}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_{\text{guid}} = 0.30$ is the guidance strength.

\textbf{Risk penalty} $\beta_{\text{risk}}$: A multi-component penalty discourages constraint-proximal and physically implausible candidates:
\begin{align}
\beta_{\text{risk}}(\btheta)
&= \omega_{\text{safe}} r_{\text{safe}}
+\omega_{\text{hard}} r_{\text{hard}}
+\omega_{\text{mono}} r_{\text{mono}},
\label{eq:risk_penalty}\\
r_{\text{safe}} &= \frac{\max(\Delta s_{\Sigma}-0.65,0)}{0.05}, \nonumber\\
r_{\text{hard}} &= \frac{\max(\Delta s_{\Sigma}-0.69,0)}{0.05}, \nonumber\\
r_{\text{mono}} &= \max(I_2-I_1,0)+\max(I_3-I_2,0). \nonumber
\end{align}
Here $\Delta s_{\Sigma} = \Delta s_1+\Delta s_2$, $\omega_{\text{safe}} = 0.20$, $\omega_{\text{hard}} = 3.00$, and $\omega_{\text{mono}} = 0.40$.

\subsubsection{Stagnation-Aware Exploration}

If the best scalarized objective has not improved for $n_{\text{stag}}$ consecutive iterations, the search width is expanded:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\text{search}} = \sigma_{\text{base}} \cdot (1 + 0.20 \cdot \min(n_{\text{stag}},\; 3))
\label{eq:stag}
\end{equation}
This encourages exploration when the optimizer is trapped in a local basin.

\subsubsection{Candidate Pool Construction}

The candidate pool combines:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{External candidates:} LLM-generated or region-sampled points (up to $n_{\text{cand}} = 15$).
\item \textbf{Internal seeds:} The current best point, plus $n_{\text{restarts}} = 12$ Gaussian samples around $\mu_{\text{search}} \pm \sigma_{\text{search}}$, and $n_{\text{random}} = 96$ uniform random samples.
\item \textbf{Optimized points:} L-BFGS-B local optimization from each internal seed (100 iterations each), maximizing $\alpha(\btheta)$.
\end{enumerate}
The top-$n_{\text{select}}$ candidates (by acquisition score, with minimum-distance deduplication) are selected for evaluation.

\subsection{Overall Algorithm}

The complete LLMBO-MO procedure is summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:llmbo-mo}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{LLMBO-MO}
\label{alg:llmbo-mo}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Simulator $\bS$, LLM $\bL$, budget $T$, warmstart count $n_{\text{ws}}$, Riesz weight set $\bW$
\Ensure Pareto front $\bP$, hypervolume trace
\State Generate Riesz $s$-energy weight set $\bW$ (cached)
\Statex \textbf{// Touchpoint 1: Warmstart with Portfolio Selection}
\State Query LLM for $n_{\text{pool}}$ initial candidates $\mathcal{C}_{\text{LLM}}$
\State Select $n_{\text{ws}}$ candidates from $\mathcal{C}_{\text{LLM}}$ via portfolio selector~\eqref{eq:portfolio}
\For{$j = 1$ to $n_{\text{ws}}$}
    \State Evaluate $\mathbf{y}_j = \bS(\btheta_j)$; add $(\btheta_j, \mathbf{y}_j)$ to database $\bD$
\EndFor
\Statex \textbf{// Main BO Loop}
\For{$t = 1$ to $T$}
    \State Sample weight $\bw^{(t)}$ from $\bW$ (without replacement)
    \State Update dynamic normalization bounds $\mathbf{y}_{\min}, \mathbf{y}_{\max}$
    \State Compute scalarized targets $g_j^{\text{tch}}$ for all $(\btheta_j, \mathbf{y}_j) \in \bD$ via~\eqref{eq:tcheby}
    \State Fit GP on $\{(\btheta_j, g_j^{\text{tch}})\}$
    \Statex \hspace{1em} \textbf{// Touchpoint 2: Iteration Guidance}
    \State Query LLM for preference (point or region mode) with confidence $c$
    \If{preference passes parsing, confidence, and feasibility guards}
        \State Build region-lifted coupling $(\lambda, \bG, \mathbf{v})$ via~\eqref{eq:lambda}
        \State Compute acquisition-time mean $\mu_{\text{c}}$ via~\eqref{eq:coupled_mean}
    \Else
        \State Set $\mu_{\text{c}} \leftarrow \mu$ (plain EI fallback)
    \EndIf
    \State Select $\btheta^* = \arg\max_{\btheta} \alpha(\btheta)$ via~\eqref{eq:acq_prior}
    \State Evaluate $\mathbf{y}^* = \bS(\btheta^*)$; add $(\btheta^*, \mathbf{y}^*)$ to $\bD$
    \State Update Pareto front $\bP$ and hypervolume
\EndFor
\State \Return $\bP$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper addressed data-limited multi-objective fast-charging design with LLMBO-MO, which introduces language-based knowledge at initialization and through bounded region preferences while leaving acquisition and simulator evaluation under numerical control. In five-seed, 56-evaluation simulations, the method matched ParEGO's mean scaled hypervolume on Chen2020 with lower variation and improved every paired Ecker2015 run, increasing the mean from 1.5866 to 1.8684. The matched ablation showed that warm starting produced the largest mean gain; the full configuration produced a similar mean with the smallest seed-to-seed deviation. These results indicate that the main repeatable benefit currently comes from screened initialization, while iteration-level guidance remains more sensitive to the run.

The study is limited to simulator evidence, two parameter sets, and five seeds, and the two cell studies use different DeepSeek-V3 variants. LLMBO-MO also required 1.74 times the ParEGO wall time in the archived Chen2020 runs. The next step is therefore physical-cell validation with recorded hardware and API latency, followed by a larger paired study that holds the LLM backend fixed across chemistries and charging conditions.
