\section{Proposed LLMBO-MO Framework}
\label{sec:method}

% 4.1 Framework Overview
\subsection{Framework Overview}

LLMBO-MO is designed to incorporate battery-domain knowledge without replacing
the numerical decision mechanism of Bayesian optimization (BO). The LLM is used
at two controlled touchpoints: it first proposes a pool of physically plausible
initial protocols, and, during the early BO iterations, it expresses a coarse
preference for a promising point or region. All objective values entering the
surrogate are obtained from the simulator; unevaluated LLM suggestions are
never inserted as observations. The next protocol is selected by scalarization,
a Gaussian-process (GP) surrogate, and expected improvement (EI), followed by
simulator evaluation and Pareto-set update.

At iteration $t$, a weight vector $\bw^{(t)}$ specifies the current objective
trade-off. The evaluated multi-objective database is re-scalarized under
$\bw^{(t)}$, a GP is fitted to the resulting scalar targets, and the LLM may
provide a point/region preference from the current optimization state. Following
the region-lifted preference principle of LGBO~\cite{ref16}, the valid
preference is converted into a mean adjustment used only during acquisition.
In the configuration evaluated in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}, this adjustment
uses GP posterior cross-covariance; no separate LLM acquisition bonus, LLM
reranking, or direct insertion of LLM-generated iteration candidates is used.
If a preference cannot be converted into a valid feasible region, the method
fails open to ordinary EI.

% 4.2 Decomposition Strategy
\subsection{Objective Transformation and Scalar Decomposition}

The three objectives in~\eqref{eq:cmop} have substantially different numerical
scales. We therefore transform charging time and capacity fade logarithmically,
while retaining the temperature-rise objective in its original scale:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\mathbf f}(\btheta)=
\begin{bmatrix}
\log_{10} t_c(\btheta) &
\Delta T_p(\btheta) &
\log_{10} Q_s(\btheta)
\end{bmatrix}^{\top}.
\label{eq:log}
\end{equation}
Let $\tilde z_i^{\ast}$ denote the corresponding transformed ideal value. For
objective $i$, the ideal-point gap is scaled as
\begin{equation}
\bar f_i(\btheta)=
\frac{\left|\tilde f_i(\btheta)-\tilde z_i^{\ast}\right|}{s_i},
\qquad
s_i=\max\!\left(y_{\max,i}-y_{\min,i},\epsilon_i\right),
\label{eq:norm}
\end{equation}
where $y_{\min,i}$ and $y_{\max,i}$ are computed from the transformed feasible
observations. The small range floor $\epsilon_i$ prevents unstable scaling when
the early observations occupy only a narrow objective interval.

Following the decomposition strategy of ParEGO~\cite{ref4}, the vector-valued
problem is converted at each iteration into an augmented Tchebycheff
subproblem:
\begin{equation}
g^{\mathrm{tch}}(\btheta\mid\bw)
=
\max_{i\in\{1,2,3\}}
\left\{w_i\bar f_i(\btheta)\right\}
+\eta\sum_{i=1}^{3} w_i\bar f_i(\btheta),
\label{eq:tch}
\end{equation}
where $\bw\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3}$, $\sum_i w_i=1$, and $\eta=0.05$. The
augmentation term reduces ties around the maximum weighted deviation.

To distribute the scalar subproblems over distinct trade-off directions, a
Riesz $s$-energy reference set is used~\cite{ref15}. For a weight set
$\bW=\{\bw^{(1)},\ldots,\bw^{(N)}\}$ on the simplex, its energy is
\begin{equation}
E_s(\bW)=\sum_{p<q}
\left\|\bw^{(p)}-\bw^{(q)}\right\|^{-s}.
\label{eq:riesz}
\end{equation}
The implementation initializes $\bW$ from a Das--Dennis simplex lattice and
applies projected repulsive-gradient relaxation to reduce~\eqref{eq:riesz},
rather than assuming that the global minimum of the energy is available in
closed form. Weight vectors are then visited without replacement before the set
is reshuffled, which provides dispersed coverage of the three-objective
trade-off space.

% 4.3 GP Surrogate Model
\subsection{Gaussian-Process Surrogate}

For the current weight vector, a single-output GP is fitted to
$\{(\btheta_j,g_j^{\mathrm{tch}})\}_{j=1}^{|\bD|}$. Each decision variable is
first mapped to $[0,1]$. The covariance model is an automatic-relevance-
determination Mat\'{e}rn-$5/2$ kernel with a white-noise term,
\begin{align}
k(\btheta,\btheta')
&=\sigma_f^2
\left(1+\sqrt{5}r+\frac{5r^2}{3}\right)e^{-\sqrt{5}r}
+\sigma_n^2\delta_{\btheta\btheta'},
\label{eq:matern}\\
r^2
&=\sum_{d=1}^{5}
\frac{(x_d-x_d')^2}{\ell_d^2},
\nonumber
\end{align}
where $x_d$ denotes the normalized coordinate and $\ell_d$ is its learned
length scale. Kernel hyperparameters are estimated by marginal-likelihood
optimization with multiple restarts. The scalar targets are standardized during
GP fitting. The posterior mean $\mu_t(\btheta)$, standard deviation
$\sigma_t(\btheta)$, and posterior covariance are subsequently used by EI and
the region-lift mechanism.

% 4.4 LLM Integration (Two Touchpoints)
\subsection{LLM Integration Through Two Controlled Touchpoints}

\subsubsection{Touchpoint 1: Constrained Warm-Start Portfolio}

The warm-start prompt provides the cell/model context, decision-variable
bounds, the hard constraint in~\eqref{eq:dsoc}, and battery-design heuristics
such as avoiding unnecessarily aggressive high-SOC charging and considering
decreasing current profiles. The LLM overgenerates a candidate pool. Candidate
responses are parsed and repaired, after which nonfinite or malformed points,
out-of-bound protocols, hard $\Delta s_1+\Delta s_2$ violations, and duplicates
are removed.

The remaining candidates are selected greedily to balance the LLM's reported
confidence, distance from the soft $\Delta s_1+\Delta s_2$ limit, monotone-current
structure, and geometric diversity. For candidate $\btheta$, define
\begin{equation}
p_{\mathrm{soft}}(\btheta)=
\frac{[\Delta s_1+\Delta s_2-s_{\mathrm{soft}}]_+}
{s_{\mathrm{hard}}-s_{\mathrm{soft}}},
\label{eq:softpen}
\end{equation}
with $s_{\mathrm{soft}}=0.65$ and $s_{\mathrm{hard}}=0.70$, and let
$\mathbb{I}_{\mathrm{mono}}(\btheta)=1$ when $I_1\ge I_2\ge I_3$. The score used
when extending a selected portfolio $\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sel}}$ is
\begin{equation}
J_{\mathrm{ws}}(\btheta)=
c_{\mathrm{ws}}(\btheta)
-\omega_p p_{\mathrm{soft}}(\btheta)
+\omega_m\mathbb{I}_{\mathrm{mono}}(\btheta)
+\omega_d d_{\min}(\btheta,\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sel}}),
\label{eq:portfolio}
\end{equation}
where $c_{\mathrm{ws}}\in[0,1]$ is the candidate confidence and $d_{\min}$ is
the minimum Euclidean distance in normalized decision space to an already
selected protocol. A small number of near-boundary probes can be retained to
avoid collapsing the initial design into only conservative schedules. If too
few valid LLM candidates remain, deterministic battery heuristics and
Latin-hypercube/random samples complete the initialization. Only after simulator
evaluation are these protocols added to $\bD$.

\subsubsection{Touchpoint 2: Weight-Conditioned Point/Region Preference}

During an early guidance window, the LLM receives the current weight vector
$\bw^{(t)}$, ideal point and objective scaling, the best scalarized observations,
recent evaluated protocols, short-horizon hypervolume feedback, boundary-failure
statistics, and feedback on the previously adopted region. The prompt explicitly
asks for a preference rather than the next experiment. The output is restricted
to either a raw-coordinate point $\btheta_{\mathrm{pref}}$, a hyper-rectangular
region $[\mathbf{lb},\mathbf{ub}]$, or no preference, together with a confidence
$c_t\in[0,1]$ and a short mechanistic rationale.

The interface checks the output format, coordinate system, direction of the
preference, and parameter bounds. A region is clipped/repaired to admissible
width and volume limits and to the $\Delta s$ safety geometry. A point
preference is converted to a small local box before the same processing. Low
confidence does not by itself create a hard rejection in the reported
region-lift variant; instead, $c_t$ directly scales the strength of the lift.
If the response is absent, unparsable, structurally invalid, or yields no
feasible anchors, its influence is set to zero and acquisition reduces to
ordinary EI.

% 4.5 GP-LLM Coupling
\subsection{Posterior-Covariance Region Lift for Battery MOBO}

The general region-lift idea follows LGBO~\cite{ref16}; here it is specialized
to a sequentially scalarized, constrained battery-charging problem and to a
posterior-cross-covariance coupling. Let a valid preferred region be represented
by $M$ feasible Sobol anchors
$\mathcal{G}=\{\mathbf g_1,\ldots,\mathbf g_M\}$. The reported configuration uses
uniform anchor weights,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf v=\frac{1}{M}\mathbf 1_M.
\label{eq:anchorweight}
\end{equation}
All quantities in the following lift are expressed in the standardized GP target
space. Let $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{GG,t}$ be the posterior covariance matrix among
the anchors. The posterior variance of the regional average is
\begin{equation}
V_{G,t}=\mathbf v^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{GG,t}\mathbf v.
\label{eq:regionvar}
\end{equation}
The confidence-normalized lift strength is
\begin{equation}
\lambda_t=
\frac{c_t}{\sqrt{\max(V_{G,t},\varepsilon_v)}},
\label{eq:lambda}
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon_v>0$ prevents numerical division by a vanishing regional
variance. For any candidate $\btheta$, let
$\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta G,t}$ denote the GP posterior cross-covariance
between $\btheta$ and the anchors. The acquisition-time mean becomes
\begin{equation}
\mu_t^{\mathrm{L}}(\btheta)=
\mu_t(\btheta)-
\lambda_t\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\theta G,t}\mathbf v,
\label{eq:shift}
\end{equation}
while the predictive variance is unchanged,
\begin{equation}
\left(\sigma_t^{\mathrm{L}}(\btheta)\right)^2
=\sigma_t^2(\btheta).
\label{eq:variance_unchanged}
\end{equation}
For this minimization problem, the negative mean shift makes points that are
posterior-correlated with the preferred region more competitive under EI. The
operation is an acquisition-time preference transformation only: it neither
modifies the training targets nor treats LLM statements as pseudo-observations.
In the reported configuration, the lift is active only during the first
$T_{\mathrm L}=16$ BO iterations; subsequent iterations use the unmodified GP
posterior.

% 4.6 Acquisition Function
\subsection{Expected Improvement and Stagnation-Aware Search}

Let $g_{\min,t}$ be the best scalarized value observed for the current weight
vector. EI is evaluated using the lifted mean when a valid coupling is active:
\begin{align}
z_t(\btheta)
&=\frac{g_{\min,t}-\mu_t^{\mathrm L}(\btheta)}
{\sigma_t(\btheta)},\label{eq:ei_z}\\
\alpha_{\mathrm{EI}}(\btheta)
&=\left(g_{\min,t}-\mu_t^{\mathrm L}(\btheta)\right)\Phi(z_t)
+\sigma_t(\btheta)\phi(z_t).
\label{eq:ei}
\end{align}
When no valid lift is available, $\mu_t^{\mathrm L}=\mu_t$, giving standard EI.
The next protocol is selected as
\begin{equation}
\btheta_{t+1}=\arg\max_{\btheta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{feas}}}
\alpha_{\mathrm{EI}}(\btheta).
\label{eq:next_theta}
\end{equation}

Acquisition optimization uses a mixed candidate pool containing the current
best protocol, Gaussian and uniform samples, and multi-start L-BFGS-B solutions
of EI. Each proposed point is repaired to satisfy the decision bounds and the
hard $\Delta s_1+\Delta s_2$ constraint. Stagnation does not add an LLM-specific
bonus to~\eqref{eq:ei}; instead, it expands the Gaussian restart dispersion,
thereby increasing exploration while preserving EI as the ranking criterion. If
EI is numerically flat over the candidate pool, the implementation falls back
to the point with largest predictive uncertainty.

% 4.7 Overall Algorithm
\subsection{Overall Algorithm}

\raggedbottom
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{LLMBO-MO}
\label{alg:llmbo}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Simulator $\bS$, LLM $\bL$, BO budget $T$, warm-start count
$n_{\mathrm{ws}}$, weight set $\bW$, guidance horizon $T_{\mathrm L}$
\Ensure Nondominated set $\bP$ and hypervolume trace
\State Generate or load the Riesz-relaxed weight set $\bW$
\State Query $\bL$ for an overgenerated warm-start pool
\State Repair/filter the pool and select $n_{\mathrm{ws}}$ protocols by
$J_{\mathrm{ws}}$
\For{each selected warm-start protocol}
    \State Evaluate $\bS$ and add the resulting observation to $\bD$
\EndFor
\For{$t=1$ to $T$}
    \State Select $\bw^{(t)}\in\bW$ and update transformed objective scaling
    \State Compute $g^{\mathrm{tch}}(\cdot\mid\bw^{(t)})$ for all feasible observations
    \State Fit the GP to the current scalarized database
    \State Set the region-lift coupling to inactive
    \If{$t\le T_{\mathrm L}$}
        \State Query $\bL$ for a point/region preference conditioned on $\bw^{(t)}$
        \State Repair the region and construct feasible Sobol anchors
        \If{the preference and regional covariance are valid}
            \State Build $\lambda_t$ and the posterior-covariance mean lift in~\eqref{eq:shift}
        \EndIf
    \EndIf
    \State Maximize EI using the lifted mean if active, otherwise plain EI
    \State Evaluate $\bS(\btheta_{t+1})$ and update $\bD$, $\bP$, and HV
\EndFor
\State \Return $\bP$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
